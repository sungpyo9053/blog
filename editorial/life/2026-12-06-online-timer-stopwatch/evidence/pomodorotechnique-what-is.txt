 What Is the Pomodoro® Technique? 
 A complete system for dealing with time. 
 Simple tools. A gentler way to plan, focus and improve your work. 

 A Different Relationship with Time 
 When deadlines approach, time can feel like an enemy. Anxiety makes it harder to focus, interruptions become more tempting, and working harder can leave us feeling further behind. 
 The Pomodoro® Technique helps you turn time into an ally . Instead of trying to control every minute, you learn to observe what happens while you work, make realistic decisions and gradually improve your process. 
 The timer is part of the practice, but the Technique is much more than a sequence of 25-minute sessions . It connects attention and breaks with planning, effort estimation, recording, reflection and teamwork. 

 Principles and Values 
 The Technique helps you build a sustainable way of working, not another system you must struggle to maintain. 

 Be Gentle with Yourself 
 Observe your energy and difficulties without blaming yourself. Breaks are part of the practice, not a reward for doing more. 

 Keep Things Simple 
 Begin with a timer, pencil and paper . Tools should make your work easier to see and organize, not add complexity. 

 Observe Before Judging 
 Record interruptions and actual effort. Compare what you planned with what happened, then make decisions using that evidence. 

 Improve a Little at a Time 
 Try small adjustments, learn from their effects and gradually build a rhythm that works for your circumstances. 

 Five Connected Processes 
 The complete method works at different levels: your relationship with time, a single activity, a day, a week and collaboration with others. 

 The Internal Process 
 Recognize how anxiety, pressure and the passage of time affect your attention. Learn to respond with awareness rather than urgency. 

 The Core Process 
 Focus on one activity, handle internal and external interruptions, take restorative breaks and observe what happens. 

 The Daily Process 
 Use the Activity Inventory and To Do Today Sheet to choose priorities, estimate effort and deal with unexpected work. 

 The Weekly Process 
 Connect daily choices with your goals, build a Weekly Timetable and review progress using the effort you actually spent. 

 The Team Process 
 Adapt the Technique to collaborative work using shared working patterns and better coordination. Respect each person's focus; the method is not a tool for monitoring people. 

 The Six Objectives 
 In the original Pomodoro® Technique book, six progressive objectives help you learn and refine the practice. 

 Find Out How Much Effort an Activity Requires 
 Observe and record the effort you actually spend. 

 Cut Down on Interruptions 
 Learn to recognize and manage interruptions from yourself and others. 

 Estimate the Effort for Activities 
 Use your records to make more realistic estimates. 

 Make the Pomodoro More Effective 
 Refine your use of focused work and breaks. 

 Set Up a Timetable 
 Organize your time and protect a sustainable rhythm. 

 Define Your Own Improvement Objective 
 Choose what to improve and learn from your results. 

 Benefits of the Pomodoro® Technique 
 As you practice the method, you can develop a more conscious approach to your work, your time and collaboration with others. 

 Focus and Concentration 
 Give one activity your attention, recognize distractions and use regular breaks to support a sustainable working rhythm. 

 More Realistic Planning 
 Record your effort, compare estimates with reality and use that information to make better decisions about your day and week. 

 A Healthier Response to Pressure 
 Make room for restorative breaks and practical choices instead of responding to every deadline by pushing yourself harder. 

 Better Team Coordination 
 Use shared working patterns and agreed moments for communication to collaborate while respecting focused work. 

 A Practice You Can Refine 
 Different tasks and circumstances call for different choices. Observe what works, adapt thoughtfully and improve your process over time. 

 Simple Tools, Working Together 

 Mechanical and software timers help you focus and remember restorative breaks. The Activity Inventory collects possible work, the To Do Today Sheet supports daily planning, and the Records Sheet helps you understand your effort. 
 The Weekly Timetable connects daily activities with longer-term goals. You can start with free eSheets and Web or PWA timers. Mechanical timers and printed kits are available for bulk orders.

 Created by Francesco Cirillo 
 Francesco began developing the Pomodoro® Technique in the 80's , while preparing for a university examination. He challenged himself to concentrate for just two minutes, using a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. Through observation and practice, that simple experiment grew into a complete method for dealing with time. 
 Its original idea continues to guide the Technique: simple tools, awareness, respect for how our minds work and gradual improvement .
