[INFO] [rosbag2_cpp]: Writing remaining messages from cache to the bag. It may take a while
[WARN] [rosbag2_cpp]: Cache buffers lost messages per topic:
	/cam: 361
Total lost: 361

This is what ros2 bag record printed on Jazzy after I stopped it. It had dropped 361 of 1,200 camera frames. It printed nothing during the 40 seconds of recording, only this, at exit. On Lyrical one more line follows: [WARN] [rosbag2_recorder]: Number of messages lost in the recorder: 427.

Short answer: the recorder drops messages when it can't write to storage as fast as they arrive. Its write cache hides the problem for the first 18 to 22 seconds, then it starts losing whole blocks of messages. The fix is to make write throughput higher than the topic data rate: a faster disk, fewer or smaller messages, or compression when the data actually compresses. A bigger --max-cache-size only delays the first drop. --max-cache-size 0 made it worse, and the recorder printed nothing at all. Best-effort topics carried over UDP lose messages before they reach the recorder, and on Humble and Lyrical nothing tells you. To be sure, count what is in the bag and compare it with what you published:

ros2 bag info my_bag            # "Count:" per topic
# expected = rate (Hz) x duration (s); or put a sequence number in each message and look for gaps

What I ran

On 2026-11-05 I ran the official ros:humble-ros-base, ros:jazzy-ros-base and ros:lyrical-ros-base images on a 2 vCPU Linux host, one distro after another. Each distro got two containers with --cpus=1.5 --memory=1500m. The second one also had --device-write-bps /dev/nvme0n1:10mb, which throttles writes to the disk that holds the bag. That is my stand-in for an SD card or a slow USB drive.

A Python publisher (pub.py) sends sensor_msgs/Image messages on /cam at a fixed rate and writes the sequence number into header.frame_id. ros2 bag record records /cam with default settings unless the row says otherwise. When the publisher finishes, the script sends SIGINT and waits for the recorder to exit. Then count.py reads the bag with rosbag2_py, counts the messages and lists the missing sequence numbers. The publisher hit its rate in every run (30.0 to 30.1 Hz, 8.0 Hz or 100.3 Hz), so every loss below happened on the recording side. Each case ran once per distro. The default storage was sqlite3 on Humble and mcap on Jazzy and Lyrical. Humble's ros-base image doesn't ship the MCAP plugin.

Cause 1: the disk is slower than the data

The slow disk takes about 10 MB/s, and 30 frames per second of 921,600 bytes is about 27.6 MB/s. The recorder keeps up at first because it writes into a memory cache. The help text explains it: "Maximum size (in bytes) of messages to hold in each buffer of cache. Default: 104857600. The cache is handled through double buffering". One buffer fills while the other is written out. When the writer falls far enough behind, incoming messages are thrown away. You can see that in where the gaps are. On Jazzy the missing frames were 611 to 701, 816 to 1048 and 1163 to 1199, so blocks of 37 to 233 frames, with good stretches between them.

In every run the recorder's own "Total lost" matched the gap count from the sequence numbers exactly, so the exit line is reliable for this cause. Its problem is timing. It comes at the very end, when the frames are already gone. rosbag2 issue #2534 asks for the count to be available during recording. Lyrical has a partial answer: it publishes rosbag2_interfaces/msg/MessagesLostEvent on /events/rosbag2_messages_lost. I echoed that topic during each Lyrical run. In the default slow-disk run it got 17 events, and their messages_lost_in_recorder fields added up to 427, the same as the exit line. Humble and Jazzy don't have this message type.

Run-to-run spread is real. On Humble the default case and the explicit -s sqlite3 case were the same configuration, and they lost 383 and 465. Read the numbers in this post as "about a third", not exact values.

Cause 2: --max-cache-size 0

Setting the cache to zero is a common suggestion, and the Pi 5 question above used it. With no cache, the recorder writes each message as it arrives. On the slow disk this lost more than the default on all three distros, and the recorder printed no warning. The warning comes from the cache, and with a size of 0 the cache is off.

Jazzy's pattern is the worst kind. The bag holds the first 594 frames and then nothing, with no error. If you set --max-cache-size 0, the only way to find out is to count.

Cause 3: best-effort QoS on a lossy transport

A camera driver often publishes best effort. By default the recorder adapts to the publisher's QoS, so it subscribes best effort too, and anything the transport drops is gone. Inside one container Fast DDS uses shared memory, so I forced it onto UDP with an XML profile (FASTRTPS_DEFAULT_PROFILES_FILE). That is closer to recording from another machine or another container. At 100 Hz with the same 921,600-byte messages:

  • Best effort: 15 lost on Humble, 256 on Jazzy and 20 on Lyrical, out of 300. Frame #0 was missing on all three. I ran this once per distro, so I can't say whether Jazzy's 256 is a real difference or a bad run.
  • Reliable: 300 of 300 on all three.

Only Jazzy printed a warning (Number of messages lost on the transport layer: 255, one less than the gap count). Lyrical's lost-event topic stayed at zero for messages_lost_in_transport. So on Humble and Lyrical, transport loss looks like a clean recording. Lyrical also warns that FASTRTPS_DEFAULT_PROFILES_FILE is deprecated in favour of FASTDDS_DEFAULT_PROFILES_FILE.

If you can change the publisher to reliable, that removed the loss here. If you can't, a reliable subscriber can't connect to a best-effort publisher. I didn't test recorder QoS overrides for this case.

Cause 4: the disk fills up

I filled /dev/shm until about 150 MB was free, then recorded 276 MB. The results were the worst of all, because two of the three distros said nothing:

  • Humble (sqlite3): terminate called after throwing an instance of 'rosbag2_storage_plugins::SqliteException', what(): Error when processing SQL statement. SQLite error (13): database or disk is full. Exit code 134. There was a 155,951,104-byte .db3 file and no metadata.yaml.
  • Jazzy (mcap): exit code 0, no error line. A 155,389,952-byte .mcap and a 0-byte metadata.yaml. rosbag2_py failed with Exception on parsing info file: invalid node; first invalid key: "version".
  • Lyrical (mcap): the same as Jazzy, with a 154,607,616-byte .mcap and a 0-byte metadata.yaml.

If a bag came out about the size of your free space, or ros2 bag info fails on it, check df first. I didn't test whether ros2 bag reindex recovers these bags.

Three things follow from this. First, a bigger cache buys time but not a full bag. Even 300 MiB per buffer lost 87 to 233 frames in 40 seconds, and stopping the recorder took up to 46.5 s while it flushed. Second, wait for the recorder to exit. In one Humble run my script killed it 30 s after SIGINT while it was still flushing. The .db3 was 850,296,832 bytes and there was no metadata.yaml, so rosbag2_py couldn't open it. Third, compression only helps when your data compresses. All-zero frames are the best case. Real camera images sit somewhere between that and random bytes, and I didn't measure them. Recording a compressed image topic instead of the raw one is the usual way to get there, but I didn't test that either.

The fix that worked every time was to get the data rate below what the storage can write. Measure the rate with ros2 topic bw (this test of ros2 topic hz and bw shows where it can be off at high rates). Then compare it with a write test on the target disk. If the rate is close to the disk speed, record to a faster disk, record fewer topics, or lower the rate or resolution.

How to check a bag for drops

  1. Read the last lines the recorder prints. Cache buffers lost messages per topic means cause 1. It is accurate, but you only get it at exit.
  2. On Lyrical, subscribe to /events/rosbag2_messages_lost during recording to see cache drops as they happen.
  3. Compare ros2 bag info counts with the rate times the duration. This also catches cause 2, cause 3 and the start-of-recording gap.
  4. If you own the publisher, add a sequence number. With one in frame_id, finding the gaps takes a short rosbag2_py script (count.py in the evidence folder).

Messages missing only at the start are a different problem: the recorder subscribed late. That is covered in missing first messages when ros2 bag record starts from a launch file.

Version notes

  • Humble (rosbag2 0.15.17): default storage sqlite3, and MCAP isn't in ros-base. A full disk crashes the recorder with an SQLite error. No transport-loss line.
  • Jazzy (rosbag2 0.26.11): default mcap. Prints Number of messages lost on the transport layer at exit. The positional topic argument still works but warns Positional "topics" argument deprecated. Please use optional "--topics" argument instead.
  • Lyrical (rosbag2 0.33.4): default mcap. Positional topics are gone (ros2: error: unrecognized arguments: /cam), so use --topics /cam. Adds Number of messages lost in the recorder and the /events/rosbag2_messages_lost topic, and has a new --max-cache-duration option. Bag files are named like 0_bag_name_2026_11_05-05_59_31.mcap.

The cache warning text was the same on all three. If you also split bags, a split can overshoot the size limit by up to the cache size on Jazzy and Lyrical, as measured in ros2 bag record split: --max-bag-size vs --max-bag-duration. Converting old .db3 bags to MCAP is covered in convert a ROS 2 .db3 bag to MCAP.

Not tested

Real SD cards or USB drives (I used a cgroup write limit), real camera images and compressed image topics, several topics at once, --storage-preset-profile, --compression-mode file, --compression-threads, recorder QoS overrides (--qos-profile-overrides-path), Cyclone DDS and Zenoh, recording across two machines, ros2 bag reindex on broken bags, snapshot mode, --max-cache-duration, and repeated runs to measure the spread.

FAQ

Why does ros2 bag record drop messages after about 20 seconds?

The write cache hides a slow disk for a while. With a 27.6 MB/s topic and a 10 MB/s disk, the first drop came at 18.2 to 21.7 s, and about a third of the frames were lost over 40 s. CPU use stays low because the recorder is waiting for the disk.

Does --max-cache-size 0 stop dropped messages?

No. On the slow disk it lost 547 to 606 of 1,200 frames, against 361 to 427 with the default, and it printed no warning on any distro.

How do I know if ros2 bag record lost messages?

For cache drops, look for "Cache buffers lost messages per topic" and "Total lost" when the recorder exits. It matched the real gap count in every run. For everything else, compare the counts in ros2 bag info with the rate times the duration, or check sequence numbers.

Will a bigger cache fix it?

It delays the first drop but doesn't stop it while the disk is slower than the data. A 300 MiB cache still lost 87 to 233 frames, and the recorder then took up to 46.5 s to exit. Wait for it to exit, or the bag can end up with no metadata.yaml.

Why are messages missing from a best-effort topic?

The recorder subscribes best effort to match the publisher, so it loses whatever the transport loses. Over UDP at 100 Hz it lost 15 to 256 of 300 messages, and a reliable publisher lost none. Only Jazzy printed a transport-loss warning.
